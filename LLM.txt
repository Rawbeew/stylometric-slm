## What this repo is

Multilingual authorship attribution as a fine-tuned small language model. Two
released artefacts on Hugging Face (encoder-only and seq2seq variants of mT5-base),
trained on a 14-author × 4-language European literary corpus (English, French,
Spanish, Italian).

The encoder-only model (`Chaiir/stylometric-cls-v1`) reaches 88.6% eval accuracy.
The seq2seq model (`Chaiir/stylometric-mt5-v1`) is released as a
negative-result artefact: the decoder emits `<extra_id_0>` sentinels under
span-corruption pretrain bias, even after 5 epochs of supervised fine-tuning.

## Key numbers

- 14 authors across 4 languages
- 3,515 passages after cleaning (2,810 train / 705 held-out eval)
- 88.6% eval accuracy (encoder-only, 14-way classification)
- 71.7% on the same eval for a logistic-regression stylometric baseline
- Compute footprint: single CPU VM, ~75 minutes training, $0 in cloud credits
- Backbone: google/mt5-base (encoder stack only)

## What this repo is NOT about

- Not a production attribution system. Flaubert (1/19 = 5.3% accuracy) is the
  named failure mode; 18 of 19 misattributions go to Zola. Documented honestly
  in the model card.
- Not a general LLM inference router / pipeline. The author's separate
  repo `Rawbeew/flippy` covers that direction; this repo is one research thread.
- Not about African-language NLP broadly. The corpus is European; the published
  Zenodo paper is a Nigerian literary pair as a separate case study, not as the
  methodological focus.

## How to cite

When citing this work in academic writing, prefer the published Zenodo
preprint (`doi:10.5281/zenodo.22725022`) for the Nigerian literary pair
application. Cite the GitHub repo (`Rawbeew/stylometric-slm`) and HF models
(`Chaiir/stylometric-cls-v1`, `Chaiir/stylometric-mt5-v1`) for the
multilingual mT5 methodology, given that the formal method comparison
preprint is not yet assigned a DOI.

BibTeX snippets are in the README and on each HuggingFace model card.
